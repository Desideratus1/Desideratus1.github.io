A couple borgillion brunches ago the Seers had an island and then they put some super fog around it and died.

Now with your history lesson out of the way, let's state for the record that I never wanted to go to Castora. I never wanted to be there, I never wanted to go near it, I never wanted to even consider it as a possibility. But Zoott apparently just can't stop himself from getting into some of the worst problems, so there I was. Hooray. But first, background.

Zoott works for the Ward. My opinions on them have been abundantly clear for as long as I knew they were a thing[^fn-ward]. Zoott informed me several months ago that he was going to be being shipped off to some shithole island on research duty, which he seemed to take as a regular assignment and I took as a particularly vicious punishment. He told me that in no uncertain terms he would bring me along, which even though it was a punishment for Zoott I wasn't really doing anything else so I considered it. Looking back, I have no idea what the hell I was thinking. Working on an island, completely at the mercy of supply ships for things like food, water, and toilet paper, and communication with the outside world is one thing, and it's not exactly a pleasant thing. Working for the Ward while doing this would make this a thousand times worse because not only would they fuck it up and you would get close to starving, but they would probably feel inconvenienced about the whole affair once they *did* arrive to fix their catastrophic mistake.

Anyway that happened and Zoott didn't take me along and instead disappeared. Usually I don't even consider this a problem because he has work, is his own person. But the promise of taking me with him, followed by his disappearance, followed by being entirely unable to contact him, followed by rumors and the news at the Arcane Ward is mobilizing in a war against the Inquest or some shit, got me thinking. And then with some cross-referencing as to where Zoott's location was I determined that he's probably in the thick of the fighting, so I resolved to go get his ass out of there. Easier said than done though, because how the hell am I going to cross an entire ocean without the support of the Ward, who I categorically refuse to cooperate with as a matter of hating them really hard?

The answer is to join the Inquest and that is what I did. No I will not be taking any questions, nor I will provide context or rationale or justifications. I did what I did, I have no regrets, and if you have a problem with this I invite you to keep it to yourself where your opinions belong.

So I packed up and joined the Inquest through a couple contacts and landed on Castora. Then I immediately joined up with a small team that was headed to reinforce Inquest positions on the island that Zoott was known to be at. The island in question, a few miles[^fn-miles] off the coast of Castora, consisted of three main parts. The whole surface was more or less owned by the Ward, who by consequence of a waypoint being established on the island had the capability for much faster travel to and from. The other two parts are two sections of a cave system, a lower and upper portion. For the upper portion, a Seneschal[^fn-seneschal] whose description I never properly learned guarded an entrance into the cave system located at the very top of the island. Another entrance to the cave system was located in a cove, also guarded by a Seneschal[^fn-seneschal-two], though by the time I arrived this one was dead and rotting not too far inside this cove entrance. I surmised the Inquest managed to kill it when they first arrived here. This section of the cave, with the dead Seneschal, was the only known entrance to an inner bastion of Seer make, the contents of which were the ultimate goal of both factions. In between the cove entrance and the bastion, in a tunnel system, was where the Inquest made their temporary home.

As a brief tangent to talk about why the Inquest were there: Beyond the whole "There is a weapon here and we shall claim it" thing that the Inquest usually involves itself in, a lot of these people were convinced to join or stay in the Inquest because they feared that the Ward would somehow take over the world if they were not stopped - and Castora was somehow the key to defeating them. You know, I can actually see where they're coming from with this one. A shadowy organization of Mists-based wizards that have been around longer than most civilizations? If I wasn't intimately familiar with the Ward being a gaggle of brainless fuckups that can't handle a god damn thing, I might have actually joined up with them to save the planet from the evil wizards.

So, the surface, owned by the Ward. The tunnel system leading to the bastion, owned by the Inquest. And then, unfortunately, a team of Ward researchers in the bastion itself, containing Zoott.

Well, fuck. Zoott was obviously in some serious danger, but I couldn't just walk up to them, blow my cover as an Inquest operative, and put myself in pretty much the same position. I resolved to wait for an opportunity, making friends with the operatives around me to pass the time and figure out what the hell everyone's plans are and what's even going on here. In all there were about twenty or so Inquest operatives in the tunnel system, including the new team that I arrived with. I placed myself as close to the monitoring stations as I could so I could keep track of Zoott and his team.

Anyway, the Ward team preoccupied itself for a few days working on the outer wards of the bastion so they could get inside, and then not a full 24 hours after they broke down the wards and got inside they got jumped and captured some towel wizards[^fn-wizards]. So that sucked. Rescue plans were made immediately, though I definitely got some weird looks from the other Inquest operatives there so I made up some bullshit about hating Zoott so damn much that I wanted to kill him myself and no one else was allowed to touch him. They bought that, thankfully.

The whole rescue plan was sort of half baked but I decided to go kill the towel wizards with kinetics rather than magic, as it seemed like the Ward's use of magic was more or less useless. Three Inquest operatives decided to join me, as I'd more or less struck up small friendships with them in the interim and somehow they convinced themselves that, through my actions, I was a super driven hero bent on saving the world from the Ward and also evil Seer shit. Mercedd, a ranged thief type. Kiia, a tall bruiser type that got close and personal. And Mazzda, who was the lead of the trio and fulfilled a more or less protector role. They were my most ardent supporters and refused to let me go in alone despite my objections that they join me.

So the four of us went in, past the gates of the bastion, and properly into the interior. What earlier always struck me as a fortress now seemed more like a grand cathedral, though obviously embedded in a cave system. A wild, overgrown garden containing a number of plant and animal species, long ornate hallways in the gaudy Seer style, archways and buttresses. After crossing the main courtyard we had three options: Two hallways disappearing further into the darkness on each side, and a massive doorway that led directly into a proper chapel, complete with altars and pews and such.

Above the altar at the far end was a large, blue, fluted crystal, pointed directly down. A Norn, suspended by his arms, stripped of clothing, and blinded, was hanging just below it as one of those towel things whipped him. So that was kind of fucking weird. It wasn't aware of my presence so I killed it easily enough and then unshackled the Norn - with the warning that, while he was Ward and I was Inquest, I'm here to get him out and as long as he didn't do some dumb shit like try and kill me, he would be walking out of here. The Inquest operatives naturally questioned this logic, as we were at war. But apparently that heroic streak they thought I had was enough to quiet their objections when I explained that my own moral obligations required me to get him out and peacefully transfer him to the Ward's lines.

At which point, Mercedd, who was standing in the back in the pews, was crushed beneath something that had stepped down from the ceiling. A lion, some twenty foot tall if it stood on all fours, seemingly made of marble with a flowing mane of blue and gold, the real protector of the complex, had executed one of our party in the blink of an eye. The three of us turned as we heard the crash, and Kiia, who had been closest, attempted to get close and strike at the beast. He was frozen in a field of stopped time, and then bisected at the waist. Two Inquest operatives dead in the span of three seconds.

I could tell immediately that this thing was in effect a chronomancer, who could project a field around him some ten feet in radius, and freeze everything in place that it touched. I never determined the make of its hide, but the look of hard stone and gold suggested to me a magically enhanced armor that would be tough to penetrate with normal small arms. And yet even with its size and the weight of its movement it was graceful, like a cannon moving as if it were a pistol. I did not have the equipment or expertise necessary to bring it down, nor even make a proper dent in its armor before I was dead. I was so hopelessly outclassed that the only remaining options were to surrender or die spitefully[^fn-armor]. Were I alone I'm not sure what I would have chosen. But of the three that went with me, one was still alive. I had already sent two men to their deaths. I could not bear to do that to a third.

Thankfully, it did not immediately come after the two of us, and I suppose now after it had thoroughly established that it was not to be fucked with, it felt like talking. And given we were not of the Ward - who it particularly hated - we were given the option of leaving. Not that it mattered much to Kiia and Mercedd. Lacking any other options I negotiated safe passage out for myself and Mazzda, as well as the Norn who had "served his purpose" already and was no longer of any use. Provided that we did not interrupt the Seneschal's work or attempt to free the rest of the Ward team, anyone in the Inquest was broadly allowed to roam how they pleased.

Thus I took the Norn back to the Ward's base of operations and went to stew in my misery. In the intervening days I would collect Kiia's corpse, as well as whatever I could possibly gather from Mercedd to cremate them. Cilla, the Inquest Overseer, didn't particularly seem to give much of a fuck, while the others had middling opinions over handing the Norn back to the Ward instead of extracting whatever information may have been useful. But by and large, Cilla allowed me to do as I pleased within reasonable bounds and eventually after wandering enough in the complex at last found Zoott and told him I was there to save him, though the rest of his team was either already dead or had since been returned to the Ward by myself.

Had I found him several days ago when they were first attempting to enter the complex I would have given him a good lecture on the dangers of fucking with dangerous magic on the frontline of a particularly vicious war, but in his state, having been a prisoner for several weeks, such a lecture would be pointless. I also learned that he was essentially being made ready to be a sort of body for possession - apparently there were Seer spirits trapped here[^fn-trapped] and the general idea was that Zoott would be possessed by the Seers, and then the Seers would be tortured even more or something. Which was pretty fucked to be honest, but Seers in general seem like crazy assholes so I guess that's par for the course[^fn-seers].

I'm sorry to say I never actually killed the Seneschal. The chance to free Zoott came when a Ward team entered the bastion to fight the Seneschal and brought in what I couldn't, heavy guns. But while they engaged the thing I went into the prison and broke Zoott out. Naturally I made a big show to the Inquest of me taking Zoott out of the caves so I could kill him in whatever way I wanted, and then once I was out of their view I sent him off and he made it off the island in one piece. I didn't bother to stay and help with the Seneschal. My work here was done, and while I was increasingly ill at ease with the potential dangers of the Ward or the Inquest having access to the Seers in the deepest reaches of the cathedral, I did what I set out to do, and prepared to leave. Cilla even gave me a goodie bag for being a good inspiration to the team.

I spoke with Mazzda not long after, feeling an obligation to tell him the truth of why his friends were dead. I was not really Inquest, I was there to save Zoott, I don't really care about the war between the Inquest and the Ward on any ideological level, et cetera. He was a true believer in the bullshit about the Inquest being freedom fighters trying to save the world from the Ward, so the betrayal he obviously felt actually kind of stung me. But he accepted it well enough a few weeks after, which I guess is some level of comfort[^fn-mazzda]. Which, for the time being, ends this section.

Look. The Inquest is undoubtedly evil, okay? A lot of them are super crazy serial killing ultra evil science types that would happily stuff people into a blender if they thought it would be entertaining, let alone scientifically useful. But when I went in there, I had an obligation to not waste the lives of people that believed in me. If we had engaged the targets, and they had fallen in battle for some good purpose, or even had the chance to fight back, maybe I wouldn't feel like I do. But I sent two men to their deaths, and none of it meant anything. I got two men killed for no reason.

Maybe I shouldn't feel bad about it. Maybe they were evil and them dying was a net good for the world. Maybe I'm just losing touch with who I'm supposed to be. I don't know. We'll see.
